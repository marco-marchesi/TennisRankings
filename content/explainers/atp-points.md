# How ATP ranking points work

The Pepperstone ATP Rankings (formerly the ATP Race / ATP Tour rankings) are
a **52-week rolling average**, not a season total. Every Monday morning,
points won 53+ weeks ago drop off, and any points won in the previous week
are added in. That's why a player's ranking can move even when they haven't
played: the table simply forgets what they did 53 weeks ago.

Here's how the system actually works.

## The two rankings

There are two parallel tables you should know:

- **ATP Live Ranking** — the official rolling 52-week table. This is what
  "the rankings" usually refers to.
- **ATP Live Race** — calendar-year points only. Resets to zero on the
  first Monday of every year. The top 8 at the end of the season qualify
  for the year-end Finals.

Both share the same per-tournament point awards.

## Points per tournament category

| Round won | Grand Slam | Masters 1000 | ATP 500 | ATP 250 | Finals (round-robin) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Winner | 2,000 | 1,000 | 500 | 250 | up to 1,500 |
| Final | 1,300 | 650 | 330 | 165 | — |
| Semi-final | 800 | 400 | 200 | 100 | — |
| Quarter-final | 400 | 200 | 100 | 50 | — |
| Fourth round | 200 | 100 | — | — | — |
| Third round | 100 | 50 | 50 | 25 | — |
| Second round | 50 | 25 | 25 | — | — |
| First round | 10 | 10 | 0 | 0 | — |

Year-end Finals scoring is non-linear: 200 per round-robin win, 400 for the
semi-final, 500 for the final, plus 1,500 for going undefeated.

## Counting: best 18 results

A player's total is the sum of their best **18** results from the rolling
52-week window. With one important wrinkle:

> **Mandatory tournaments count whether the player turns up or not.**

The four Grand Slams and eight of the nine Masters 1000 events are
mandatory. Skip one (without a valid medical reason) and a zero is locked
into the "best 18" — you literally cannot get rid of it. This is why
top-20 players hardly ever skip a Masters 1000 by choice.

## Why points expire

This is the source of most of the surprise movement you'll see on this site.

Imagine player A won Roland Garros 2025 — that's 2,000 points. For the
next 52 weeks, those 2,000 points sit in their total. The **week before
Roland Garros 2026**, those 2,000 points fall off the rolling window.
Whatever they earn at Roland Garros 2026 replaces them.

If they win again, net change = 0. If they lose in the first round, net
change = −2,000 points and they can drop a dozen places overnight.

That's why **defending champions are always at risk** going into the
tournament they previously won. Our[ranking projection page](/rankings/projection)
makes this explicit: it shows points expiring in the next four weeks for
every top-100 player.

## Race to Finals

In parallel, every win contributes to the **ATP Race**. The Race resets to
zero on the first Monday of the calendar year, and tracks only points won
in *this* season. At the end of the year, the top eight by Race points
play the year-end Finals.

Why two rankings? Because the rolling 52-week system rewards consistency
across seasons, but the Finals is a season-long competition. The Race
gives the season its own narrative arc.

## Quick reference: rules of thumb

- **Best 18 results from the last 52 weeks.**
- **Mandatory tournaments always count**, even if you skip.
- **Points expire 52 weeks after they were won**, not on a fixed date.
- **The ATP Race is a separate, calendar-year-only table.**
- **You can lose ranking without playing** — that's just points rolling off.
